# Midnight Detectives: Hollow Creek
## 13+ Creative and Product Design Standard

**Status:** Approved creative direction  
**Applies to:** Narrative, characters, supernatural entities, environments, puzzles, UI, audio, marketing, and future gameplay  
**Purpose:** Establish a consistent general-audience mystery experience suitable for YouTube Playables' 13+ audience expectations. This is a product-design standard, not an official age rating or a substitute for Google's certification review.

---

## 1. Audience and positioning

- **Intended audience:** General audience, age 13 and older.
- **Positioning:** A cinematic supernatural mystery adventure for teens and a broad general audience, featuring young protagonists.
- Do not position, package, advertise, or design the game as a game specifically for young children, a preschool experience, or “just for kids.”
- The protagonists being 12 years old does not change the intended audience by itself. The complete presentation—including mechanics, art direction, language, themes, marketing, metadata, and promotion—must consistently support the general-audience positioning.
- 13+ does not mean graphic violence, gore, or adult content. Suspense, mystery, uncertainty, and psychological unease are the preferred tools.

## 2. Core experience

The player is an investigator, not a form-filler. The experience should emphasize:

1. **Explore:** Read the town and its spaces; notice details that matter.
2. **Observe:** Inspect objects, symbols, traces, testimony, and environmental changes.
3. **Connect:** Compare evidence and identify relationships or contradictions.
4. **Hypothesize:** Form a theory and test it against what has been discovered.
5. **Discover:** See the world respond and uncover the next layer of the mystery.

Puzzles should reward observation, inference, experimentation, and evidence-based reasoning. Avoid repetitive quiz screens, arbitrary guessing, shallow tap sequences, or long text-entry tasks as the primary form of play.

## 3. Character and canon integrity

The established four young detectives remain the central team:

- Ethan Parker
- Noah Brooks
- Lily Carter
- Maya Williams

Each is 12 years old. This is the current approved character canon. Their age does not need to match the game's intended audience age: the game is designed for a general audience aged 13+. Preserve their established identities, relationships, personalities, and approved visual references.

The existing supporting cast, family relationships, pets, and approved character references remain governed by the project's canonical registries and source materials. Do not silently promote prototype-only characters into canon.

### Pets

Buddy, Whiskers, Piper, Clover, and Nibbles are established pets. Preserve their individual identities. Do not give them human speech or invent human-like dialogue for them.

## 4. Supernatural entity standards

The supernatural entities are core parts of Hollow Creek's identity. They must be treated as distinct, memorable presences—not interchangeable monsters, comic villains, mascots, or generic fantasy enemies.

### ANT-001 — The Hollow Man

**Established visual identity**
- Weathered hat
- Pale mask
- Tattered charcoal coat

**Established presence**
- Silent
- Watchful
- Psychological and unsettling

**Creative direction:** Use distance, framing, stillness, partial visibility, environmental cues, and uncertainty to create dread. Do not add a voice, origin, powers, weakness, or backstory unless approved through the canon process.

### ANT-002 — The Shadow Walker

**Established visual identity**
- Faceless hood
- Violet eyes or violet energy
- Torn shadow-like fabric
- Elongated claws

**Established presence**
- Silent
- A supernatural threat
- More physically threatening in presentation than The Hollow Man

**Creative direction:** Use silhouette, controlled movement, violet accents, shadow distortion, and carefully timed appearances to create tension. Keep its visual language distinct from The Hollow Man. Do not add a voice, origin, powers, weakness, or backstory unless approved through the canon process.

### Entity continuity rules

- Preserve each entity's approved silhouette, key visual identifiers, and reference images across scenes, assets, and generations.
- Never merge their designs or transfer signature features between them.
- Avoid cute, toy-like, slapstick, or mascot-like interpretations.
- Prefer suspense and psychological/supernatural tension over graphic violence or gore.
- A new ability, origin, motivation, weakness, or canonical event requires explicit creator approval and a registry update.
- An entity's appearance should serve story, atmosphere, or meaningful gameplay—not function as an arbitrary jump scare.

## 5. Visual direction

**Target:** Cinematic atmospheric stylized realism.

- Build a believable town with layered spaces, readable landmarks, material detail, weather, and purposeful environmental storytelling.
- Use controlled lighting, fog, shadow, and warm/cool contrast to support mystery.
- Characters should feel like believable young people with agency and distinct personalities, not toy-like mascots.
- Supernatural elements may be heightened and stylized, but must remain coherent with the grounded world.
- Avoid preschool-cartoon proportions, nursery color palettes, toy packaging aesthetics, and overly cheerful mascot presentation.
- Maintain visual clarity: important clues, routes, interaction points, and threats must remain legible on mobile screens.

Mood references are directional only. They do not become canonical assets unless explicitly approved.

## 6. Narrative and tone

- Core tone: intrigue, secrets, uncertainty, supernatural unease, emotional stakes, and discovery.
- Let the player question what they have seen and compare competing explanations.
- Use character encounters and environmental storytelling to reveal information progressively.
- Keep dialogue purposeful, concise, and natural. Do not explain every clue immediately.
- Use silence and environmental sound deliberately, especially around supernatural entities.
- Avoid graphic depictions of violence, gore, cruelty, or shock content. Threat can be communicated through implication, atmosphere, pacing, and consequence.
- Do not invent unresolved lore. In particular, keep unresolved identities, entity origins, and unregistered mechanisms unresolved until approved.

## 7. Puzzle and interaction standards

A puzzle should be understandable through the world and satisfying to solve.

Preferred loop:
1. The player notices a visual or environmental clue.
2. The game gives a clear, optional contextual hint when needed.
3. The player manipulates meaningful in-world objects or mechanisms.
4. Feedback communicates whether the action changed the mechanism.
5. A correct solution creates a visible or audible world-state change and reveals new information.

Requirements:
- Teach interaction through demonstration and feedback rather than long instructions.
- Use plain, concise language without talking down to the player.
- Avoid relying on tiny symbols, color alone, obscure wording, or unexplained arbitrary sequences.
- Make wrong attempts informative and recoverable; do not punish experimentation with confusing resets.
- Ensure puzzle solutions follow evidence the player could reasonably discover.
- Use multi-step reasoning, evidence chains, environmental clues, and conflicting accounts where appropriate.
- Do not turn every interaction into a dialogue form, multiple-choice quiz, or journal-filling exercise.

## 8. UI, accessibility, and mobile usability

- Keep the interface cinematic, focused, and readable.
- Use clear hierarchy, high contrast, scalable text, and comfortably sized touch targets.
- Never rely on color alone to communicate clue state, correctness, danger, or progress.
- Keep controls and prompts clear of each other and of important scene content.
- Support touch and mouse interactions consistently, with responsive layouts across aspect ratios.
- Preserve progress and interaction state through supported resize, pause, and resume behavior.
- Provide clear feedback for selection, interaction, puzzle state, and completion.
- Accessibility and usability must be validated against the current YouTube Playables requirements; this document does not replace those requirements.

## 9. Audio and atmosphere

- Use restrained environmental layers: wind, distant town ambience, room tone, mechanism sounds, and carefully placed supernatural cues.
- Give important interactions clear audio feedback without making audio essential to understanding the puzzle.
- Respect the platform's audio-enabled state and pause/resume lifecycle.
- Avoid constant loud stingers or repetitive jump-scare effects.
- Provide a coherent experience when audio is disabled.

## 10. Marketing and presentation

- Describe the product as a cinematic mystery or supernatural mystery adventure for a broad general audience.
- Do not use child-directed positioning, metadata, thumbnails, or promotional language.
- Do not use misleading thumbnails or imply content that is not in the game.
- Use only original, creator-owned, or properly licensed visual, audio, character, and brand assets.
- Ensure store/portal metadata and in-game presentation accurately describe the experience and comply with current platform rules.

## 11. Canon and change control

This standard does not authorize new canon. The project's canonical registries and approved reference materials remain the source of truth.

Before a new character, entity attribute, location, mechanism, relationship, power, weakness, or historical event is treated as canon:
1. Identify the proposed addition and why the story/game needs it.
2. Check it against existing registries and unresolved items.
3. Obtain explicit creator approval.
4. Update the appropriate registry and reference material.
5. Apply the approved detail consistently across narrative, assets, and gameplay.

Prototype-only content must remain clearly labeled as prototype material until approved.

## 12. Design review gate

Before expanding gameplay or approving a major asset/feature, review it against these questions:

- Does it support a general 13+ audience rather than being designed specifically for young children?
- Does it strengthen the cinematic mystery identity?
- Does it preserve established character and entity canon?
- Does it make the player investigate, reason, or discover something meaningful?
- Is the interaction clear and fair on a mobile touch screen?
- Does it create suspense through atmosphere and story rather than graphic content?
- Is it accessible, readable, and understandable without relying on audio or color alone?
- Does it comply with the current YouTube Playables technical, design, privacy, accessibility, trust-and-safety, and certification requirements?

A “no” or “unknown” answer requires review before the feature is treated as production-ready.
